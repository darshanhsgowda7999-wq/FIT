## Fitness basics

Fitness is more than looking good. It is how well your heart, lungs, muscles and mind handle everyday life. Five areas make up a complete routine.

Heart & lungs

### Cardio endurance

Running, cycling, swimming or brisk walking. Aim for 150 minutes of moderate cardio a week to protect your heart.

Power

### Muscular strength

Lift or push against resistance 2–3 days a week. Strength also protects your bones and joints as you age.

Stamina

### Muscular endurance

How long your muscles can keep working. Push-ups, planks and high-rep sets build it.

Range

### Flexibility & mobility

Stretching and mobility drills keep your joints moving well and lower injury risk.

Composition

### Body composition

The balance of fat to lean mass matters more than scale weight. Track waist size, photos and strength too.

Foundation

### Recovery

Muscles grow while you rest. Sleep 7–9 hours and take at least one lighter day each week.

## CrossFit & burn workouts

CrossFit-style training mixes weightlifting, gymnastics and cardio in short, intense sessions. Intense workouts burn a lot of calories in little time. Always warm up for 5–10 minutes first, and scale the moves to your level.

### Fat-burn AMRAP

20 minutes · As many rounds as possible

- 10 air squats
- 10 push-ups
- 10 sit-ups
- 10 burpees

### Cindy

20 minutes · Classic benchmark

- 5 pull-ups (or ring rows)
- 10 push-ups
- 15 air squats
- Repeat for 20 minutes

### Engine builder

5 rounds for time

- 400 m run
- 15 kettlebell swings
- 12 box jumps
- 9 dumbbell thrusters

### Tabata blast

4 minutes · 20s on, 10s off, 8 rounds

- Choose one: jump squats, mountain climbers, high knees or burpees
- Give maximum effort every round

### Strength + burn

Every minute on the minute, 12 minutes

- Min 1: 5 deadlifts
- Min 2: 10 wall balls or squat throws
- Min 3: 12 calorie row or ski erg

### Bodyweight burner

No equipment · 3 rounds for time

- 20 lunges
- 15 push-ups
- 20 jumping jacks
- 30 s plank

## More fitness information

Small habits often matter more than perfect plans. Tap a question to read more.

How much water should I drink?

A good start is 30–35 ml per kg of body weight daily (about 2–3 litres for most adults). Drink more when you sweat or the weather is hot. Pale yellow urine is a simple sign you are hydrated.

How important is sleep?

Sleep is when your body repairs muscle and balances hunger hormones. Short sleep raises cravings and lowers performance. Aim for 7–9 hours at a consistent time.

How often should I work out?

Beginners do well with 3 full-body strength sessions plus 2 cardio sessions a week. Add days only when you recover well. Consistency over months beats intensity for a few weeks.

Can I target fat loss in one area (spot reduction)?

No. You cannot choose where your body burns fat. A calorie deficit lowers fat across the whole body, and genetics decide the order. Crunches strengthen abs but do not remove belly fat on their own.

Should I take supplements?

Food comes first. Supplements with good evidence are protein powder (for convenience), creatine monohydrate (3–5 g a day) and vitamin D if you are deficient. Talk to a doctor if you have a medical condition.

How do I avoid injuries?

Warm up, learn proper form before adding weight, progress slowly (about 5–10% more load per week) and stop if you feel sharp pain. Soreness is normal; sharp or joint pain is not.

What if I plateau?

Check your sleep, recalculate calories at your new weight, increase training difficulty (more weight, reps or sets) and give any change 2–3 weeks before judging it. Taking a lighter “deload” week can also help.

When should I see a doctor?

Get medical advice before starting if you have heart, joint or blood-pressure problems, are pregnant, or have not exercised for a long time. Stop and seek help for chest pain, dizziness or unusual shortness of breath.
